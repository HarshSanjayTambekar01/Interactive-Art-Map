The Tree of Many Hands

An original artwork that fuses three regional painting traditions — the geometric dance of Warli, the fine-pen intricacy of Kalamkari, and the bold double-lined colour of Madhubani — into a single tree of life.

Artwork by Harsh Sanjay Tambekar · Reg. No. RA2411030010223

Artist's statement

The Tree of Many Hands fuses three regional grammars of Indian painting onto one canvas. At its heart stands the Kalamkari Tree of Life — its trunk cross-hatched with fine pen strokes, its branches tipped with paisley leaves and red lotus blooms, in the indigo, madder and mustard of natural dyes.

Around its roots, on terracotta earth, the Warli people dance their eternal tarpa spiral — geometric white figures of circles and triangles — while a plain-circle moon watches over the night.

And radiating through the whole is Madhubani colour: a sun with a smiling face and bold rays, double-outlined fish in a blue pond, a dot-patterned peacock, and a triangle border band that frames the composition like a painted wall.

Three traditions, three ways of seeing — one story: that life is a circle, and art is its witness.

— Harsh Sanjay Tambekar, RA2411030010223

Three traditions, one canvas

Warli

Maharashtra · tribal wall art

White geometric figures of circles, triangles and squares painted on terracotta-ochre mud walls. Signature motifs: the tarpa dance spiral, stick-figure people, huts, and the cycle of nature.

Kalamkari

Andhra Pradesh · pen & natural dyes

Fine pen (kalam) drawing coloured with natural indigo, madder red and mustard yellow. Signature motifs: the Tree of Life, peacocks, paisley leaves, lotus and stippled detail.

Madhubani

Bihar (Mithila) · wall-to-paper art

Bold double outlines, dense patterning and bright mineral colour. Signature motifs: a radiant sun with a face, fish, peacocks, lotus, and patterned border bands.
